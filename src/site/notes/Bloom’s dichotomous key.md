根據 Semsar 與 Casagrand（2017）的研究，BDK（Bloom’s Dichotomous Key）評估的標的同時包含「試題（考題）」與「課程材料（Course Materials）」

## 評分流程

評估試題時，評估者依序回答以下「Yes/No」問題並進行跳轉：

第一階段：基礎檢索與低階技能分類

- 問題 1：學生能否直接背誦（Memorize）出該特定問題的答案？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 2。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 4。
- 問題 2：回答時，學生是否幾乎完全重現課堂或教材（講義、課本、作業等）中聽過或看過的內容？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 記憶（Remember）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 3。
- 問題 3：學生是否透過用自己的話表達、將範例與概念配對、或改變呈現形式來展現概念理解？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 理解（Comprehend）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 返回問題 1。

第二階段：多元解答判斷（高階技能分流）

- 問題 4：該問題是否存在多於一種潛在有效解法/答案（More than one valid solution）？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 5（引導至高階思維分流）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 8。
- 問題 5：學生是否在進行價值判斷並/或為自己的答案提出合理論證？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 評鑑（Evaluate）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 6。
- 問題 6：學生是否將資訊整合為整體大方向，或創造出課堂未見過的新事物（新假說、新模型）？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 綜合/創造（Synthesize/Create）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 7。
- 問題 7：學生是否被要求對資訊進行比較或對比（Compare/Contrast）？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 分析（Analyze）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 16。

第三階段：數據解讀與運算分支

- 問題 8：學生是否需要解讀數據（Data interpretation）（如圖表、數據、情境故事題）？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 9（數據分支）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 14。
- 問題 9：是否要求評估數據與情境是否一致、或批判實驗數據與方法的有效性/質量？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 評鑑（Evaluate）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 10。
- 問題 10：是否根據數據建立模型或提出新假說？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 綜合/創造（Synthesize/Create）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 11。
- 問題 11：是否對數據意義歸納出結論，或必須自數據中區分相關與無關資訊？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 分析（Analyze）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 12。
- 問題 12：是否利用數據計算出特定變項的數值？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 應用（Apply）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 13。
- 問題 13：是否僅重新描述數據以證明理解其代表意義？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 理解（Comprehend）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 返回問題 4 與問題 8。

第四階段：剩餘題型分類

- 問題 14：是否將多個領域的資訊結合以創造新模式/結構/模型？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 綜合/創造（Synthesize/Create）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 15。
- 問題 15：是否預測簡單情境變化後的結果或趨勢？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 應用（Apply）。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 前往問題 16。
- 問題 16：是否以不同於課堂見過的形式（新範例、類比等）轉化概念以展現理解？
    - Yes $\rightarrow$ 理解（Comprehend）16。
    - No $\rightarrow$ 返回各類別描述交叉核對。


## 原始題目

- Question 1. Could students memorize the answer to this specific question? Yes: go to question 2. No: go to question 4. 
- Question 2. To answer the question, are students repeating nearly exactly what they have heard or seen in class materials (including lecture, textbook,  laboratory, homework, clicker, etc.)? Yes ¡ See Remember No: go to question 3. 
- Question 3. Are students demonstrating a conceptual understanding by putting the answer in their own words, matching examples to concepts, representing  a concept in a new form (words to graph, etc.), etc.? Yes ¡ See Comprehend  No: Go back to question 1. If you are sure the answer to question 1 is yes, the question should fit into “remember” or “comprehend.” 
- Question 4. Is there potentially more than one valid solution* (even if a “better” one exists or if there is a limit to what solutions can be chosen)? Yes: go to question 5. No: go to question 8. 
- Question 5. Are students making a judgment and/or justifying their answer? Yes ¡ See Evaluate No: go to question 6. 
- Question 6. Are students synthesizing information into a bigger picture (coherent whole) or creating something they haven’t seen before (a novel  hypothesis, novel model, etc.)? Yes ¡ See Synthesize/create No: go to question 7.  
- Question 7. Are students being asked to compare/contrast information? Yes ¡ See Analyze  No: go to question 16.† 
- Question 8. To answer the question, do students have to interpret data (graph, table, figure, story problem, etc.)? Yes: go to question 9. No: go to question 14. 
- Question 9. Are students determining whether the data are consistent with a given scenario or whether conclusions are consistent with the data? Are  students critiquing validity, quality, or experimental data/methods? Yes ¡ see Evaluate No: go to question 10. 
- Question 10. Are students building up a model or novel hypothesis from the data? Yes ¡ See Synthesize/create No: go to question 11. 
- Question 11. Are students coming to a conclusion about what the data mean (they may or may not be required to explain the conclusion) and/or having to  decide what data are important to solve the problem (i.e., picking out relevant from irrelevant information)? Yes ¡ See Analyze No: go to question 12. 
- Question 12. Are students using the data to calculate the value of a variable? Yes ¡ See Apply  No: go to question 13. 
- Question 13. Are students redescribing the data to demonstrate they understand what the data represent? Yes ¡ See Comprehend  No: go back to questions 4 and 8. 
- Question 14. Are students putting information from several areas together to create a new pattern/structure/model/etc.? Yes ¡ See Synthesize/create No: go to question 15. 
- Question 15. Are students predicting the outcome or trend of a fairly simple change to a scenario? Yes ¡ See Apply  No: go to question 16. 
- Question 16. Are students demonstrating that they understand a concept by putting it into a different form (new example, analogy, comparison, etc.) than  they have seen in class? Yes¡ See Comprehend No: go back through each category or refer to category descriptions to see which fits the best
